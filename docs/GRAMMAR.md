# Grammar and format conventions

## Table columns

Use Form / feature, Value, Norwegian example, and English translation. The example must actually use the listed form. For alternatives separated by /, give one sentence for each alternative, in matching order, or split the alternatives into separate rows. Repeated spellings across grammatical cells are normal: god is both masculine and feminine.

A gender, person, number label or usage construction is a feature rather than a new word form. Invariant words still receive a contextual example. For a genuinely inapplicable form, put — and explain it; no fabricated example is required. Use Unverified for an unknown form and retain it in the review list.

## Nouns

Entall = singular; flertall = plural; ubestemt = indefinite; bestemt = definite. Feminine nouns may also permit masculine inflection in Bokmål. Learn accepted articles and complete paradigms. Plural-only words and mass nouns need usage notes rather than mechanical plural generation. Productive genitives can be added with -s, or an apostrophe after a word ending in s, x or z; verify the exact construction and meaning.

## Verbs

Norwegian finite verbs do not change by person or number. Compound-tense rows demonstrate constructions in sentences. A future expressed with skal can convey a plan or obligation; vil can express prediction or volition. Conditional ville + infinitive and ville ha + past participle should be illustrated in an appropriate conditional context. Skulle + infinitive can express a future seen from a past viewpoint; do not automatically label every use conditional.

Show active and passive forms only where the verb and construction permit them. S-passive can share spelling in infinitive and present; separate the syntactic contexts. Agreement of a past participle used as an adjective is separate from its use in a perfect tense. Reflexive pronouns depend on the subject; an imperative normally requires deg/dere rather than a mechanically retained seg.

## Adjectives and adverbs

Adjectives agree with gender, number and definiteness. Give comparative and both superlative forms when applicable. Mer/mest is available for many gradable adjectives, but absolute meanings and idiomatic restrictions matter. Do not force comparison for every adjective. Adverbs are generally invariable, though some permit comparison. Fort in the adverb model is an adverbial use of a dictionary-listed adjective.

## Pronouns and determiners

Pronouns can have subject and object forms. For han, ham and han are accepted objects. Seg is a related reflexive pronoun, not a case form of han. Possessives such as min are treated as determiners here. Min/mi/mitt/mine agrees with the possessed noun. Show an appropriate example for each agreement form.

## Invariable words and expressions

Prepositions, coordinating conjunctions, subordinating conjunctions and interjections normally use an invariant word plus contextual constructions. The subordinate-clause example with fordi places ikke before the finite verb in neutral order. In a multiword expression, identify which element changes and keep required complements visible. Only add whole-expression paradigms that are justified by actual usage.

## Numerals

This project gives numerals a learning category. Cardinal to is invariable. Related ordinal andre is a separate word and is labelled as related, rather than presented as inflection of to. Bokmålsordboka classifies ordinal andre as an adjective.

## Status and provenance

Verified refers to the cited forms or reviewed sense, not to all conceivable uses. Derived means a productive construction or explicitly explained compound formation. Unverified means a check is still needed. The 4,472-entry reference snapshot keeps its original statuses and limitations; it does not already contain an example for each form. The 12 worked examples demonstrate the enriched format. Norwegian teaching sentences and their English translations were authored for this project.

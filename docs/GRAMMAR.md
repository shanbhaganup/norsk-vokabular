# Grammar and format conventions

## Form tables

| Rule | Guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Columns | Use **Form / feature**, **Value**, **Norwegian example**, **English translation**. The example must use the listed form. |
| Alternatives | For values separated by `/`, give a matching example and translation for every alternative in the same order, or put each alternative in its own row. |
| Features | Gender, person, number and usage constructions describe a form or its use; they are not extra word forms. Repeated spellings across form rows are normal (for example, *god* is both masculine and feminine). |
| Invariable words | Give a contextual example even when the word itself does not change. |
| Inapplicable / unknown | Use `—` only when a form genuinely does not apply and explain why; no example is needed. Use `Unverified` when a form is unknown, and add it to the review list. |

## Form labels in English

| Word type | Norwegian labels and English meanings |
| --- | --- |
| Noun | • `kjønn / artikkel` — gender and indefinite article.<br>• `entall ubestemt` — singular, an unspecified item.<br>• `entall bestemt` — singular, a specific or known item.<br>• `flertall ubestemt` — plural, unspecified items.<br>• `flertall bestemt` — plural, specific or known items. |
| Verb | • `infinitiv` — infinitive: base form, usually with “to”.<br>• `presens` — present: current, habitual or general actions.<br>• `preteritum` — simple past.<br>• `perfektum partisipp` — past participle.<br>• `presens partisipp` — present participle.<br>• `imperativ` — imperative: a command or request.<br>• `presens perfektum` — present perfect: a past action linked to the present.<br>• `preteritum perfektum` — past perfect: an action completed before another past event.<br>• `futurum` — future: a plan, prediction or intention.<br>• `futurum perfektum` — future perfect: an action completed by a future time.<br>• `kondisjonalis` — conditional: a hypothetical or conditional action.<br>• `kondisjonalis perfektum` — perfect conditional: what would have happened.<br>• `framtid sett fra fortiden` — future in the past: an action still future from a past viewpoint.<br>• `passiv` — passive: the subject receives the action. `s-passiv` uses an `-s` form; `bli-passiv` uses `bli` with a past participle. |
| Adjective | • `positiv` — base form, before comparison.<br>• `hankjønn`, `hunkjønn`, `intetkjønn` — masculine, feminine and neuter agreement.<br>• `flertall` — plural agreement.<br>• `bestemt` — form used with a definite noun or determiner.<br>• `komparativ` — comparative, “-er” / “more”.<br>• `superlativ ubestemt` — indefinite superlative, “-est” / “most”.<br>• `superlativ bestemt` — definite superlative, “the -est” / “the most”. |
| Adverb | • `grunnform / positiv` — base form describing how, when, where or to what degree.<br>• `komparativ` — comparative, “-er” / “more”.<br>• `superlativ` — superlative, “-est” / “most”. |
| Pronoun | • `personlig pronomen` — personal pronoun.<br>• `person` — first (speaker), second (person addressed) or third (other).<br>• `tall` — singular or plural.<br>• `subjektsform` — subject form, used for the doer or topic of the clause.<br>• `objektsform` — object form, used after verbs or prepositions.<br>• `refleksivt pronomen` — refers back to the clause’s subject. |
| Determiner | • `hankjønn`, `hunkjønn`, `intetkjønn` — masculine, feminine and neuter forms.<br>• `flertall` — plural form. |
| Preposition | • `ordklasse` — word class; identifies a preposition.<br>• `bøyning` — inflection; usually marked invariable.<br>• `konstruksjon, sted / tid` — place or time phrase formed with its complement. |
| Conjunction / subjunction | • `konjunksjon` — coordinating conjunction, linking words or clauses of equal status.<br>• `subjunksjon` — subordinating conjunction, introducing a subordinate clause.<br>• `konstruksjon, ord` — linked words or phrases.<br>• `helsetning` — main clause that can stand as a sentence.<br>• `leddsetning` — subordinate clause dependent on a main clause.<br>• `ubøyelig` — invariable; the word does not change form. |
| Interjection | • `interjeksjon` — interjection, a short exclamation or social expression.<br>• `bruk` — communicative function, such as a greeting.<br>• `ubøyelig` — invariable; the word does not change form. |
| Expression | • Verb-like labels (such as `presens` or `preteritum`) describe the tense or form of the whole expression.<br>• `konstruksjon` — how the expression combines with other words or complements.<br>• `passiv` — passive construction, if the expression permits one. |
| Numeral | • `grunntall` — cardinal number used for counting (“one”, “two”, etc.).<br>• `kjønnsbøyning` — agreement with the gender or number of what is counted, where applicable.<br>• `ordenstall` — ordinal number expressing order or position (“first”, “second”). |

## Word-type conventions

| Word type | Conventions |
| --- | --- |
| Nouns | Include the accepted article/gender and all four singular/plural, indefinite/definite forms. Feminine nouns may also allow masculine inflection in Bokmål; preserve accepted alternatives. For plural-only words and mass nouns, explain usage rather than mechanically generating a paradigm. Productive genitives may use `-s` or an apostrophe after final `s`, `x` or `z`; verify the construction and meaning. |
| Verbs | Finite verbs do not change by person or number. Compound-tense rows show constructions in context. `skal` can express a plan or obligation; `vil` can express prediction or volition. Illustrate `ville` + infinitive and `ville ha` + past participle in suitable conditional contexts. `skulle` + infinitive can express a future viewed from the past; do not label every use conditional. Include active/passive only where permitted. S-passive may share spelling in infinitive and present, so distinguish the syntax. Keep participle agreement as an adjective separate from participle use in compound tenses. Reflexive pronouns depend on the subject; imperatives normally use `deg` / `dere`, not mechanically retained `seg`. |
| Adjectives and adverbs | Adjectives agree with gender, number and definiteness. Give comparative and indefinite/definite superlative when applicable. Many gradable adjectives allow `mer` / `mest`, but absolute meanings and idiom can restrict comparison; do not force it. Adverbs are generally invariable, though some compare. *Fort* in the adverb model is an adverbial use of a dictionary-listed adjective. |
| Pronouns and determiners | For *han*, both *ham* and *han* are accepted object forms. *Seg* is a related reflexive pronoun, not a case form of *han*. Treat possessives such as *min* as determiners; *min / mi / mitt / mine* agrees with the possessed noun. Exemplify each applicable form. |
| Prepositions, conjunctions, subjunctions and interjections | These are normally invariable; illustrate their constructions and use. In a subordinate clause with *fordi*, neutral word order puts *ikke* before the finite verb. For multiword expressions, show which element changes and include required complements; include a whole-expression paradigm only when supported by actual usage. |
| Numerals | Numerals are a learning category in this project. Cardinal *to* is invariable. Related ordinal *andre* is a separate word, not an inflection of *to*; Bokmålsordboka classifies it as an adjective. |

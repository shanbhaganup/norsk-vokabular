## leke

**English:** to play; to pretend to be or do something

**Topics:** sport, childhood, play

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitiv | å leke | Barna liker å leke ute etter skolen. | The children like to play outside after school. |
| Presens | leker | Hun leker med dukker på rommet sitt. | She plays with dolls in her room. |
| Preteritum | lekte | Vi lekte sisten i friminuttet. | We played tag during recess. |
| Perfektum partisipp | lekt | Barna har lekt i hagen hele dagen. | The children have played in the garden all day. |
| Presens perfektum | har lekt | De har lekt sammen siden barnehagen. | They have played together since preschool. |
| Preteritum perfektum | hadde lekt | Hun hadde lekt ferdig før middagen. | She had finished playing before dinner. |
| Futurum | skal leke | Vi skal leke ute etter lunsj. | We are going to play outside after lunch. |

| Futurum perfektum | vil ha lekt | Innen kvelden vil de ha lekt alle spillene. | By evening, they will have played all the games. |

**Form status:** Dictionary forms checked. Core play sense is intransitive; passive rows illustrate object-bearing “play/perform” contexts and are less common than active use.

**Notes:** *Leke* can mean engage in play, pretend to be something (*leke sjørøver*), or engage superficially with a matter (*leke med tanken*). S-passive is most natural in game/sport contexts, while the bli-passive examples use the sense “act out/perform.” Past and participle form is *lekte/lekt*. Adjectival uses are context-specific; examples are authored, and the dictionary’s inflection table supports *lekt* and definite/plural *lekte*.

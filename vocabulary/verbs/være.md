## være

**English:** to be; to exist; to be located

**Topics:** general, identity

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitiv | å være | Det er godt å være hjemme. | It is good to be at home. |
| Presens | er | Jeg er klar for møtet. | I am ready for the meeting. |
| Preteritum | var | Han var lærer i mange år. | He was a teacher for many years. |
| Perfektum partisipp | vært | Hun har vært i Bergen før. | She has been to Bergen before. |
| Presens perfektum | har vært | Det har vært en travel uke. | It has been a busy week. |
| Preteritum perfektum | hadde vært | Vi hadde vært der én gang før. | We had been there once before. |
| Futurum | skal være / vil være | Jeg skal være hjemme i kveld. / Jeg vil være hjemme i kveld. | I will be home tonight. / I want to be home tonight. |
| Futurum perfektum | skal ha vært / vil ha vært | Innen fredag skal hun ha vært her i en uke. / Innen fredag vil hun ha vært her i en uke. | By Friday she will have been here for a week. / By Friday she will have been here for a week. |
| Kondisjonalis | ville være / skulle være | Jeg ville være med hvis jeg kunne. / Hun sa at hun skulle være her klokka ni. | I would come along if I could. / She said she would be here at nine. |

**Form status:** Dictionary headword and listed forms checked; productive constructions and original examples reviewed.

**Notes:** The dictionary lists vært only as a neuter participle form; masculine/feminine and definite/plural adjectival participle rows are inapplicable in the cited paradigm. Værende is dictionary-listed but rare; the example “sto værende” is grammatical though formal. Passive is inapplicable to this copular/intransitive verb. The dictionary lists neuter vært, but its adjectival use in this sense is uncertain and marked Unverified. Passive forms do not apply to this copular/intransitive use. Compound constructions are examples, not dictionary inflections.

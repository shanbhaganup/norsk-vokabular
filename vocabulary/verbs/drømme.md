## drømme

**English:** to dream (while asleep); to dream of or aspire to something

**Topics:** sleep, emotions, aspirations

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitiv | å drømme | Jeg liker å drømme om framtiden. | I like to dream about the future. |
| Presens | drømmer | Hun drømmer ofte om havet. | She often dreams about the sea. |
| Preteritum | drømte | Jeg drømte om deg i natt. | I dreamed about you last night. |
| Perfektum partisipp | drømt | Det var en drømt ferie. | It was a dreamt-of holiday. |
| Presens perfektum | har drømt | Vi har drømt om dette lenge. | We have dreamed about this for a long time. |
| Preteritum perfektum | hadde drømt | Hun hadde drømt om reisen før hun fikk råd til den. | She had dreamed about the trip before she could afford it. |
| Futurum | skal drømme / vil drømme | I natt skal jeg drømme om en lang ferie. / Jeg vil drømme meg tilbake til barndommen. | Tonight I am going to dream about a long holiday. / I want to dream myself back to childhood. |
| Futurum perfektum | skal ha drømt / vil ha drømt | Innen sommeren skal hun ha drømt om reisen i flere måneder. / Om noen år vil han ha drømt om dette lenge. | By summer she will have been dreaming about the trip for months. / In a few years he will have dreamed about this for a long time. |

**Form status:** Inflection verified in the reference record; dictionary lemma and senses checked. Context-dependent passive and adjectival participle examples are authored teaching examples.

**Notes:** Bokmålsordboka gives drømme both for having dreams during sleep and for imagining or longing for something. The past and participle are drømte and drømt. Passive forms are grammatical in general impersonal or marked contexts but are less common than active formulations. Adjectival drømt often has the sense “dream” or “dreamed-of”; the examples use that interpretation.

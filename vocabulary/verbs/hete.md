## hete

**English:** to be called or named

**Topics:** General / unassigned

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitiv | å hete | Det er fint å hete noe som er lett å uttale. | It is nice to have a name that is easy to pronounce. |
| Presens | heter | Hun heter Mari og bor i Tromsø. | Her name is Mari and she lives in Tromsø. |
| Preteritum | het | Hva het restauranten vi besøkte i går? | What was the name of the restaurant we visited yesterday? |
| Preteritum | hette | Han het Erik før han tok et nytt navn. | His name was Erik before he took a new name. |
| Perfektum partisipp | hett | Hun har hett det samme hele livet. | She has had the same name all her life. |
| Presens perfektum | har hett | Stedet har hett Lillevik siden det ble grunnlagt. | The place has been called Lillevik since it was founded. |
| Preteritum perfektum | hadde hett | Gata hadde hett Kirkebakken før navneendringen. | The street had been called Kirkebakken before the name change. |
| Futurum, skal | skal hete | Den nye skolen skal hete Solsida skole. | The new school will be called Solsida School. |
| Futurum, vil | vil hete | Jeg tror barnet vil hete Nora. | I think the child will be named Nora. |
| Futurum perfektum, skal | skal ha hett | Innen sommeren skal firmaet ha hett Nordlys i ti år. | By summer the company will have been called Nordlys for ten years. |

**Form status:** Bokmålsordboka verifies the headword and cited forms. Compound tenses and applicable passive constructions are shown. Examples are original.

**Notes:** This entry teaches hete 3 III, “have as a name.” The snapshot combines forms from hete 2 II, “heat,” and hete 3 III. The name sense has past forms het/hette, participle hett, and definite/plural adjectival form hette. The heat-sense forms heta/hetet and their participles belong to a separate dictionary entry and are excluded. The verb is intransitive, so passive constructions are inapplicable. The dictionary lists the imperative het and present participle hetende, but natural examples in the name sense could not be verified. Attributive use of the past participle is also unverified.

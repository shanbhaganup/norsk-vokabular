## drive

**English:** to drive/force, drift, run or operate (a business or machine), engage in an activity

**Topics:** work, transport, general

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitiv | å drive | Hun liker å drive en liten kafé. | She likes running a small café. |
| Presens | driver | De driver et verksted sammen. | They run a workshop together. |
| Preteritum, alternative 1 | dreiv | Han dreiv spaden ned i jorda. | He drove the spade down into the soil. |
| Preteritum, alternative 2 | drev | Han drev spaden ned i jorda. | He drove the spade down into the soil. |

**Form status:** Dictionary forms verified; compound tenses and passive examples are productive constructions.

**Notes:** Bokmålsordboka lists both *dreiv* and *drev* in the past tense, and *drevet* as perfect participle. Its masculine/feminine participle table gives *dreven*, while neuter is *drevet* and definite/plural *drevne*. *Dreven* also commonly means skilled or experienced; the example uses that lexicalized adjectival sense. The infinitive has several distinct dictionary senses: driving something in, being carried by wind/current, running an enterprise, and regularly engaging in an activity. This entry summarizes these uses; it does not claim that every construction is natural in every sense.

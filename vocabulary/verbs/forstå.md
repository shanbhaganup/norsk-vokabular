## forstå

**English:** to understand, comprehend; to understand or sympathize with someone

**Topics:** communication, learning, relationships

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitiv | å forstå | Det er lett å forstå reglene med et eksempel. | The rules are easy to understand with an example. |
| Presens | forstår | Jeg forstår hvorfor du er bekymret. | I understand why you are worried. |
| Preteritum | forsto | Hun forsto spørsmålet med en gang. | She understood the question at once. |
| Preteritum | forstod | Han forstod hva læreren mente. | He understood what the teacher meant. |
| Perfektum partisipp | forstått | Beskjeden ble forstått av alle. | The message was understood by everyone. |
| Presens perfektum | har forstått | Vi har forstått instruksjonene. | We have understood the instructions. |
| Preteritum perfektum | hadde forstått | Hun hadde forstått svaret før timen var slutt. | She had understood the answer before the lesson ended. |
| Futurum | skal forstå / vil forstå | Du skal forstå dette etter øvelsen. / Jeg tror de vil forstå sammenhengen. | You are going to understand this after the exercise. / I think they will understand the connection. |
| Futurum perfektum | skal ha forstått / vil ha forstått | Innen fredag skal klassen ha forstått regelen. / Innen da vil hun ha forstått hva som skjedde. | By Friday the class will have understood the rule. / By then she will have understood what happened. |

| Presens partisipp | forstående | En forstående kollega lyttet uten å avbryte. | An understanding colleague listened without interrupting. |

**Form status:** Dictionary lists the core forms and both past-tense alternatives. Tenses, passives and participial agreement are shown in original constructions.

**Notes:** Covers both comprehension (“grasp”) and understanding/empathizing with a person. The fixed construction *forstå seg på* (“know about/be good at”) and the causative construction *la en forstå* (“make someone understand indirectly”) are related usages, not inflected forms. Conditional rows use *ville/skulle* alternatives as recorded in the reference; their meanings can depend on context. Examples are authored teaching examples.

## trett

**English:** tired; weary; sleepy; fed up

**Topics:** health, emotions

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positiv hankjønn | trett | Han var trett etter den lange arbeidsdagen. | He was tired after the long workday. |
| Positiv hunkjønn | trett | Hun ble trett av den lange gåturen. | She became tired from the long walk. |
| Positiv intetkjønn | trett | Barnet virket trett etter reisen. | The child seemed tired after the trip. |
| Positiv flertall | trette | De trette spillerne satte seg på benken. | The tired players sat down on the bench. |
| Positiv bestemt entall | trette | Den trette syklisten hvilte ved veien. | The tired cyclist rested by the road. |
| Komparativ | trettere | Jeg føler meg trettere i dag enn i går. | I feel more tired today than yesterday. |
| Superlativ ubestemt | trettest | Etter fjellturen var jeg trettest av alle. | After the mountain hike, I was the most tired of everyone. |
| Superlativ bestemt | tretteste | Hun var den tretteste deltakeren etter løpet. | She was the most tired participant after the race. |

**Form status:** Agreement and both comparison paradigms verified in Bokmålsordboka; original teaching examples.

**Notes:** Bokmålsordboka accepts both *trett* and *trøtt*, with separate agreement and comparison spellings. The sense can mean lacking energy, sleepy, or tired of something; examples here use physical tiredness or sleepiness. Source records *word-0356* (*trett*) and *word-0362* (*trøtt*) point to these spelling variants of the same adjective, so they share this canonical entry.

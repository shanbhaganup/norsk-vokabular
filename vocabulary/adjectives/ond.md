## ond

**English:** evil; wicked; malicious

**Topics:** emotions, describing

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positiv hankjønn | ond | Fortellingen handler om en ond konge. | The story is about an evil king. |
| Positiv hunkjønn | ond | Hun møtte ei ond heks i skogen. | She met an evil witch in the forest. |
| Positiv intetkjønn | ondt | Det var et ondt rykte. | It was a malicious rumor. |
| Positiv flertall | onde | De onde planene ble avslørt. | The wicked plans were exposed. |
| Positiv bestemt entall | onde | Den onde trollmannen forsvant. | The evil wizard disappeared. |
| Komparativ | ondere | I denne boka blir skurken stadig ondere. | In this book, the villain grows increasingly evil. |
| Komparativ | verre | Situasjonen ble verre etter sviket. | The situation became worse after the betrayal. |
| Superlativ ubestemt | ondest | Av personene i fortellingen var han ondest. | Of the characters in the story, he was the most evil. |
| Superlativ ubestemt | verst | Hun mente at sviket var verst av alt. | She thought the betrayal was the worst of all. |
| Superlativ bestemt | ondeste | Han spilte den ondeste skurken i filmen. | He played the most evil villain in the film. |
| Superlativ bestemt | verste | Dette var det verste av de onde utfallene. | This was the worst of the evil outcomes. |

**Form status:** Dictionary agreement and all listed comparison alternatives checked; alternative comparatives have different idiomatic ranges.

**Notes:** The dictionary defines *ond* as evil/malicious. The source gloss “worst” belongs to comparison, not the positive sense. Ordbøkene lists both regular *ondere/ondest/ondeste* and suppletive *verre/verst/verste*; *verre/verst/verste* are common general comparatives for “worse/worst” and do not always mean “more evil.”

## velkommen

**English:** welcome; gladly received

**Topics:** social interaction, hospitality

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positiv hankjønn | velkommen | Hver gjest er velkommen til å bli med. | Every guest is welcome to join. |
| Positiv hunkjønn | velkommen | Hun følte seg velkommen i den nye gruppa. | She felt welcome in the new group. |
| Positiv intetkjønn | velkomment | Det var et velkomment avbrekk i arbeidsdagen. | It was a welcome break in the workday. |
| Positiv flertall | velkomne | Alle besøkende er velkomne på møtet. | All visitors are welcome at the meeting. |
| Positiv bestemt entall | velkomne | Den velkomne gjesten fikk en varm mottakelse. | The welcome guest received a warm reception. |
| Komparativ (productive construction) | mer velkommen | Etter hjelpen følte han seg enda mer velkommen. | After the help, he felt even more welcome. |
| Superlativ ubestemt (productive construction) | mest velkommen | Av de nye ansatte følte hun seg mest velkommen. | Of the new employees, she felt the most welcome. |
| Superlativ bestemt (productive construction) | mest velkomne | Dette var den mest velkomne nyheten den uka. | This was the most welcome news that week. |

**Form status:** Agreement verified; mer/mest are productive constructions because no synthetic comparison is listed. Original teaching examples.

**Notes:** Means appreciated as a visitor or being gladly received. The dictionary also covers something one is very pleased to have, such as *en velkommen fridag* (“a welcome day off”). The adjective also appears in greetings (*velkommen hit!*); this fixed greeting is not an inflected form.

## lei

**English:** unpleasant; tiresome; fed up

**Topics:** emotions

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positiv hankjønn | lei | Han er lei av den lange ventingen. | He is tired of the long wait. |
| Positiv hunkjønn | lei | Hun er lei av den samme sangen. | She is fed up with the same song. |
| Positiv intetkjønn | leit | Et leit mas holdt oss våkne. | Tiresome nagging kept us awake. |
| Positiv flertall | leie | Vi ble leie av møtet. | We grew tired of the meeting. |
| Positiv bestemt entall | leie | Den leie beskjeden ble utsatt. | The unpleasant message was delayed. |
| Komparativ | leiere | Denne historien er leiere enn den første. | This story is more unpleasant than the first one. |
| Superlativ ubestemt | leiest | Av de to historiene var denne leiest. | Of the two stories, this one was the most unpleasant. |
| Superlativ bestemt | leieste | Dette var den leieste historien hun hadde hørt. | This was the most unpleasant story she had heard. |

**Form status:** Bokmålsordboka verifies agreement and synthetic comparison.

**Notes:** The reference gloss “tired” corresponds especially to the dictionary sense of being bored or fed up, often used with av, illustrated in the masculine, feminine, and plural examples. The neuter, definite, and comparison examples use the related dictionary sense “causing discomfort; unpleasant” so those forms have natural adjectival examples. Bokmålsordboka also lists related senses including being sad or weary.

## isolert

**English:** isolated, cut off from the surroundings

**Topics:** General / unassigned

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positiv hankjønn | isolert | De slo seg ned på en isolert fjellgård. | They settled on an isolated mountain farm. |
| Positiv hunkjønn | isolert | Ei isolert bygd mistet veiforbindelsen i uværet. | An isolated village lost its road connection in the storm. |
| Positiv intetkjønn | isolert | Familien bodde på et isolert småbruk uten bilvei. | The family lived on an isolated smallholding with no road access. |
| Positiv flertall | isolerte | Isolerte gårder fikk forsyninger med båt. | Isolated farms received supplies by boat. |
| Positiv bestemt entall | isolerte | Den isolerte fjellgården hadde ingen naboer i nærheten. | The isolated mountain farm had no neighbors nearby. |
| Komparativ | mer isolert | Bygda ble mer isolert etter at ferga sluttet å gå. | The village became more isolated after the ferry stopped running. |
| Superlativ ubestemt | mest isolert | Av de tre småbrukene er det øverste mest isolert. | Of the three smallholdings, the highest one is in the most isolated location. |
| Superlativ bestemt | mest isolerte | Den mest isolerte bygda har bare båtforbindelse. | The most isolated village can only be reached by boat. |

**Form status:** Positive agreement follows the past-participle paradigm in Bokmålsordboka. Comparison with mer/mest is productive in this sense. Examples are original.

**Notes:** Source spelling isolert. Agreement follows the past participle of isolere. The reference labels this record food, but its gloss gives no basis for a food-specific sense. The examples use geographical isolation, where there can be degrees of remoteness. The dictionary also lists et isolert tilfelle, ‘an isolated case’, for an occurrence considered on its own.

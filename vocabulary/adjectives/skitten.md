## skitten

**English:** dirty; filthy; improper; underhanded

**Topics:** describing, morality

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positiv hankjønn | skitten | Han hadde en skitten jakke etter turen. | He had a dirty jacket after the trip. |
| Positiv hunkjønn | skitten | Hun fant ei skitten skjorte i sekken. | She found a dirty shirt in the bag. |
| Positiv intetkjønn | skittent | Barnet hadde et skittent ansikt. | The child had a dirty face. |
| Positiv flertall | skitne | De skitne koppene står på kjøkkenet. | The dirty cups are in the kitchen. |
| Positiv bestemt entall | skitne | Den skitne genseren må vaskes. | The dirty sweater needs washing. |
| Komparativ | skitnere | Arbeidsbuksene ble skitnere utover dagen. | The work trousers got dirtier as the day went on. |
| Superlativ ubestemt | skitnest | Av alle skoene var mine skitnest. | Of all the shoes, mine were the dirtiest. |
| Superlativ bestemt | skitneste | Dette er den skitneste koppen i skapet. | This is the dirtiest cup in the cupboard. |

**Form status:** Dictionary agreement and comparison checked; original teaching examples.

**Notes:** Besides physically dirty, the adjective can mean morally or sexually improper, or morally/legally dubious (for example, a dirty trick or campaign). Examples here use the literal cleanliness sense; forms apply across these senses.

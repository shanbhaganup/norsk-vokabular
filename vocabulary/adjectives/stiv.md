## stiv

**English:** stiff; rigid; formal; steep (prices)

**Topics:** describing, condition

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positiv hankjønn | stiv | Han hadde en stiv nakke etter reisen. | He had a stiff neck after the trip. |
| Positiv hunkjønn | stiv | Hun tok ei stiv skjorte ut av skapet. | She took a stiff shirt out of the closet. |
| Positiv intetkjønn | stivt | Det var et stivt smil på bildet. | There was a stiff smile in the picture. |
| Positiv flertall | stive | De stive støvlene trengte å gås inn. | The stiff boots needed breaking in. |
| Positiv bestemt entall | stive | Den stive kragen klødde hele kvelden. | The stiff collar itched all evening. |
| Komparativ | stivere | Denne pappen er stivere enn den tynne. | This cardboard is stiffer than the thin one. |
| Superlativ ubestemt | stivest | Av materialene er dette stivest. | Of the materials, this is the stiffest. |
| Superlativ bestemt | stiveste | Han valgte den stiveste fjæra. | He chose the stiffest spring. |

**Form status:** Dictionary agreement and comparison checked; original teaching examples.

**Notes:** The dictionary includes physical stiffness, something setting or becoming stiff, immobility, stiffness from soreness or fear, an unnatural manner, stubbornness, skill (for example, *stiv i fransk*), unreasonably high prices, strong weather, and “a full” period of time. Examples focus on physical or figurative rigidity. The source gloss “stiff” covers the core sense but not all usages.

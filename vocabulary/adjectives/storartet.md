## storartet

**English:** magnificent; wonderful; splendid

**Topics:** describing, evaluation

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positiv hankjønn | storartet | Vi hadde en storartet kveld sammen. | We had a wonderful evening together. |
| Positiv hankjønn, alternative | storarta | De så en storarta forestilling. | They saw a magnificent performance. |
| Positiv hunkjønn | storartet | Det var ei storartet utsikt fra toppen. | There was a magnificent view from the summit. |
| Positiv hunkjønn, alternative | storarta | Det var ei storarta utsikt fra toppen. | There was a magnificent view from the summit. |
| Positiv intetkjønn | storartet | De fikk et storartet tilbud. | They received a splendid offer. |
| Positiv intetkjønn, alternative | storarta | De fikk et storarta tilbud. | They received a splendid offer. |

| Positiv flertall, alternative | storarta | De storarta planene ble godt mottatt. | The splendid plans were well received. |

| Positiv bestemt entall, alternative | storarta | Den storarta utsikten tok pusten fra oss. | The magnificent view took our breath away. |
| Komparativ (productive construction) | mer storartet | Den andre konserten var mer storartet enn den første. | The second concert was more magnificent than the first. |
| Superlativ ubestemt (productive construction) | mest storartet | Av opplevelsene var dette mest storartet. | Of the experiences, this was the most wonderful. |
| Superlativ bestemt (productive construction) | mest storartede | Det var den mest storartede feiringen de hadde hatt. | It was the most splendid celebration they had had. |

**Form status:** Agreement alternatives checked; mer/mest shown as productive constructions; original teaching examples.

**Notes:** The dictionary headword includes both *storartet* and *storarta*. Its paradigms also include plural/definite *storartede* and *storartete*. The reference lists only *storartet* and *storartede/storartete*; the accepted *storarta* series has been added and each variant is exemplified. Meaning: impressive or splendid. The dictionary also lists an adverbial use; that use is not an adjective form. Degree forms are productive mer/mest constructions, not synthetic dictionary forms.

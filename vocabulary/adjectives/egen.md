## egen

**English:** stubborn; obstinate

**Topics:** family

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positiv hankjønn | egen | Han er egen og furten. | He is stubborn and grumpy. |
| Positiv hunkjønn | egen | Hun er egen og furten. | She is stubborn and grumpy. |
| Positiv intetkjønn | egent | Barnet var egent og sta. | The child was stubborn and obstinate. |
| Positiv flertall | egne | De egne naboene sa tydelig fra. | The stubborn neighbors spoke plainly. |
| Positiv bestemt entall | egne | Den egne naboen sa tydelig fra. | The stubborn neighbor spoke plainly. |
| Komparativ | egnere | Han ble egnere etter hvert. | He became more stubborn over time. |
| Superlativ ubestemt | egnest | Han er egnest av alle. | He is the most stubborn of all. |
| Superlativ bestemt | egneste | Den egneste mannen nektet å gi seg. | The most stubborn man refused to give in. |

**Form status:** Dictionary agreement and comparison forms verified for the adjective sense “stubborn.”

**Notes:** The reference gloss “own” does not match the adjective entry: Bokmålsordboka classifies *egen* meaning “own” as a determinative, while the adjective *egen* means “stubborn/obstinate.” This working adjective entry follows the verified adjective sense. The “own” sense belongs under determinatives.

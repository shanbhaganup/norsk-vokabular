## slem

**English:** mean; naughty; nasty; difficult

**Topics:** behavior, describing

| Form / feature | Value | Norwegian example | English translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positiv hankjønn | slem | Han var slem mot lillebroren sin. | He was mean to his little brother. |
| Positiv hunkjønn | slem | Hun gjorde en slem ting mot venninnen. | She did a mean thing to her friend. |
| Positiv intetkjønn | slemt | Det var et slemt knep. | It was a nasty trick. |
| Positiv flertall | slemme | De slemme barna måtte si unnskyld. | The naughty children had to apologize. |
| Positiv bestemt entall | slemme | Den slemme kommentaren såret ham. | The mean comment hurt him. |
| Komparativ | slemmere | Han ble slemmere da de andre lo. | He became meaner when the others laughed. |
| Superlativ ubestemt | slemmest | Av søsknene var hun slemmest mot broren. | Of the siblings, she was the meanest to her brother. |
| Superlativ bestemt | slemmeste | Det var den slemmeste kommentaren i hele samtalen. | It was the meanest comment in the whole conversation. |

**Form status:** Dictionary agreement and comparison checked; original teaching examples.

**Notes:** The dictionary covers deliberately causing harm or pain, being naughty, being difficult or unpleasant, being bad, and being very skilled at an activity (as in *slem til å lyve*). Examples illustrate several senses. The source English gloss “naughty” is narrower than the dictionary meaning.
